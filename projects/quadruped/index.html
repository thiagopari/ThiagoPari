Overview

A 12-DOF quadruped built on Will Cogley's RoboFox mechanical design. My work: 3D printing and assembly, electronics and power integration, servo calibration, and the kinematics and gait code in a MuJoCo model. It stood stably on calibrated servos in February 2026.

Status (Sep 2026): the legs are being reprinted; IK/FK will then be tuned against the real hardware before walking gaits move from simulation to the robot.

Mechanical Design

The mechanical design is Will Cogley's RoboFox; all structural parts are 3D printed. Each leg has 3 degrees of freedom, using a four-bar linkage with 1:1 gears.

  • Four-bar leg linkage with 75 mm links, re-modeled in OpenSCAD to check dimensions for the kinematics
  • Vibration-isolated IMU mount using foam interface for sensor protection

MuJoCo model of the quadruped (simulation)

Electronics Integration

Embedded system with Raspberry Pi 4 for high-level control, communicating with servo drivers and sensors over I2C.

Power Distribution

  • Dual XL4015 buck converters providing 5V (logic) and 6V (servos) rails
  • Power budget calculated from total servo current draw with safety margin
  • 2S LiPo battery selected based on space and weight constraints
  • Decoupling capacitors for servo current spike protection

Communication Architecture

  • I2C bus shared between PCA9685 PWM driver and BNO055 IMU
  • PCA9685 controlling all 12 Corona DS-843MG servos via PWM
  • FSR402 force sensors on feet for ground contact detection

CAD render of the RoboFox frame with the electronics layout

Calibration & Testing

Systematic hardware validation before integration, including individual component testing and servo limit discovery.

  • Tested voltage rails to verify buck converter output; identified and replaced one defective unit (outputting 14V instead of 5.9V)
  • Mapped safe angular limits for each servo to prevent mechanical interference
  • Validated BNO055 IMU readings and calibration status
  • Tested FSR402 force sensors for reliable ground contact detection
  • Tuned balance parameters for stable standing posture

Kinematics and Gaits in MuJoCo

The kinematics and gait code are developed against a MuJoCo model first, then tuned on the physical robot.

  • MJCF model (12 DOF, 4 legs × 3 joints) sized from the robot's 75 mm links and 260 mm body
  • IK (closed-form hip roll, Newton–Raphson for pitch and knee) and FK for the four-bar legs, checked with an FK–IK round-trip test
  • Gait controller (trot, walk, pace, bound) with Bézier swing trajectories, in simulation
  • Next: tune IK/FK against the reprinted hardware
