Real vs. simulated: Everything runs in an NVIDIA Isaac Sim twin of Boston’s Charles River Dam. The thermal camera is simulated, and the defect flags come from ground truth.

The Problem

Massachusetts has 778 bridges that need an underwater inspection, and MassDOT has 7 divers. The water is so murky that divers often work by touch, scour is worst in a flood, when nobody can go in, and federal rules keep the diver: sonar can only supplement. So what if a robot went first, and divers went only where the map says?

How It Works

  1. Above the water, a drone flown by real ArduPilot SITL maps the canal wall with a 128-beam lidar, a gimbal camera and a simulated thermal camera. Under the deck, 12 GPS satellites drop to 7.
  2. At the waterline, the drone flags damage and hands the spot to the robot below.
  3. Below, a BlueROV2 Heavy on ArduSub has no GPS, so it navigates by a simulated DVL (about 0.4 m drift over 60 m). Its sonar finds a missing granite block, a scour pit and exposed timber piles.
  4. Lidar and sonar join into one 3D model above and below the water. Every finding lands in one dashboard with the camera footage, the GPS track and a report written by Kiro that waits for an engineer to sign off.

My Part

The bridge triage, specced in Kiro before any code: it ranks all 5,252 Massachusetts bridge records one row per bridge (5,226 bridges), and 33 tests pass.
